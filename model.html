01alice.py · bob.py

Qubits and measurement

A qubit is the pair (bit, basis). alice.prepare(n) returns two uint8 arrays of length n, one of bits and one of bases. Basis 0 is Z (rectilinear) and basis 1 is X (diagonal). No state vector or amplitude is stored anywhere in Layer 1.

Basisbit = 0bit = 1
Z 0|0⟩|1⟩
X 1|+⟩|−⟩

The measurement rule

All of the quantum behaviour in the model comes from one function, bob.measure(). It compares the basis a qubit carries with the basis it is measured in. Eve's measurement calls the same function.

The rule is exact for BB84's four states. Measuring a state in its own basis gives the encoded bit with probability 1. Measuring it in the other basis gives each outcome with probability |⟨0|+⟩|² = |⟨1|+⟩|² = 1/2, and the same holds for |−⟩. Those are the only two cases BB84 ever produces, so a basis comparison and one fair random bit reproduce the Born-rule outcome distribution exactly. No amplitudes are needed.

The cost is one comparison and one random bit per qubit. The full quantum stage (Alice, Eve, noise, loss, Bob and sifting) for 105 qubits takes a median of 3.0 ms (20 runs, runner.run_quantum_stage). That is what lets the experiments run 50 trials per point.

02runner.run_quantum_stage

What happens to each qubit, in order

runner.run_quantum_stage() applies the stages below in this fixed order. Each tag gives the stage's effect on the QBER of the sifted key.

  1. 1

    Alice

    alice.prepareadds no error

    Draws n bits and n bases, each independent and uniform. The two arrays are both the qubits she sends and her private record for sifting.

  2. 2

    Eve

    eve.intercept_resendQBER f/4

    Each qubit is intercepted independently with probability f (rng.random(n) < f). For an intercepted qubit Eve picks a uniform random basis, measures with bob.measure, and replaces the qubit with (her bit, her basis). Qubits she does not intercept pass through unchanged.

    The re-preparation is what causes errors. Her basis is wrong with probability 1/2. The qubit then reaches Bob in the wrong basis, and on a sifted position his result is a coin flip, wrong with probability 1/2. So QE = f × 1/2 × 1/2 = f/4.

  3. 3

    Noise

    channel.depolarizeQBER p

    Flips the carried bit with probability p, independently of basis and of every other qubit. It is applied after Eve. With no Eve the sifted QBER is p.

    The function is named for depolarizing noise, but its parameter is the bit-error rate the channel causes, not the depolarizing parameter. A channel ρ → (1 − λ)ρ + λI/2 produces an error rate of λ/2 in both bases, so p corresponds to λ/2.

  4. 4

    Loss

    channel.transmitQBER unchanged

    Each qubit reaches Bob independently with probability η. channel.transmit returns the mask of survivors. run_quantum_stage then keeps only those positions on both sides, standing in for Bob announcing which slots produced a detection.

    Loss does not depend on the bit or the basis, so it removes positions without changing the error rate among the survivors. Measured at p = 5% with 400,000 qubits: η = 1 gives 200,075 sifted bits at 4.94%, and η = 0.5 gives 100,300 sifted bits at 5.07%.

  5. 5

    Bob

    bob.receive · sifting.siftadds no error

    Picks a uniform random basis for each received qubit and measures with the same rule. Sifting keeps the positions where his basis equals Alice's, about half of them. On those positions any disagreement with Alice comes from stages 2 and 3.

Composition rule

Errors do not add

Q = QE + p − 2·QE·p

A sifted bit is wrong when exactly one of the two sources flipped it. If both flipped it, the flips cancel. Eve's error and the noise flip are independent in this model, so

Q = QE(1 − p) + (1 − QE)p

which expands to the formula above. Worked example: full intercept-resend (f = 1, so QE = 0.25) on a channel with p = 0.02.

QE × (1 − p) = 0.25 × 0.98Eve's error survives, noise did not flip0.245 (1 − QE) × p = 0.75 × 0.02noise flips a bit Eve left correct0.015 Q = 0.245 + 0.0150.260

The same result from the formula: 0.25 + 0.02 − 2 × 0.25 × 0.02 = 25% + 2% − 1% = 26%, not the 27% that adding the rates would give. Simulated (f = 1, p = 0.02, 199,760 sifted bits): 26.07%.

03sifting.py · estimation.py · security.py · reconcile.py · amplify.py

Classical post-processing

StageModuleWhat the code does
Siftingsifting.py sift() keeps positions where Alice's and Bob's bases match. disclose_sample() then draws m sifted positions at random without replacement for estimation and removes them from the key. m = 2,000 by default (PipelineConfig.sample_size).
Estimationestimation.py Point estimate Q̂ = k/m from the disclosed sample, with a Wilson score interval (95% by default). The bounds are pinned to exactly 0 when k = 0 and exactly 1 when k = m. Wilson is used because the Wald interval has zero width at Q̂ = 0.
Decisionsecurity.py r(Q) = 1 − 2h(Q), evaluated at min(Q, 1/2). Abort when r(Q̂) ≤ 0. The threshold is the root of r(Q) = 0, found by bisection on (0, 1/2) with tolerance 10−12: 11.0028%. No threshold constant is written in the code.
Reconciliationreconcile.py Four block-parity passes. The first block size is round(0.73/Q̂), clamped to [4, n], and it doubles each pass. Passes 2 to 4 use a fresh public permutation. Each block with mismatched parity is binary-searched and one error is flipped. Then comes random-subset confirmation: each bit is included with probability 1/2, and a mismatched subset is bisected and corrected. It stops after 20 consecutive agreeing checks, or reports failure after 2,000 checks.
Amplificationamplify.py Toeplitz hashing. The m × n matrix comes from a public seed of m + n − 1 bits, and the product is computed by FFT convolution reduced mod 2. Output length m = floor(r(Q̂)·n). Raises KeyMismatchError if the two input keys differ.

Measured from arrays

Runs on real bits

  • Reconciliation works on the actual sifted arrays. The code tracks diff = alice ^ bob and computes each parity comparison as the parity of diff over the compared positions. That equals comparing the two announced parities. It returns Bob's corrected key as an array.
  • Hashing applies the same public Toeplitz seed to Alice's array and to Bob's array. The two final keys can be compared bit for bit, and the demo prints both.
  • Diagnostics come from these arrays: leakage (every disclosed parity bit), the residual error count, and the efficiency leakage / (n·h(e)), where e is the realised error rate before reconciliation.

Computed from r(Q)

Derived from the formula

  • The key length m = floor(r(Q̂)·n) is calculated, not measured. It sets how many rows the Toeplitz matrix has.
  • The abort decision and the 11.0028% threshold come from the same r(Q).
  • Leakage is not subtracted from m. The two h(Q) terms in r already pay for error correction and for privacy amplification.
  • Eve's remaining information about the hashed key is never computed. The length is a bound that holds under the assumptions below.

Model assumptions

Stated in the security.py docstring. The rate formula and the threshold hold only under these.

  1. Asymptotic regime: no finite-size correction to r(Q).
  2. One-way post-processing.
  3. Collective attacks.
  4. Ideal single-photon source.
  5. The same QBER in the Z and X bases.

The threshold is a property of these assumptions, not a constant of BB84. With two-way post-processing the tolerable QBER rises to about 18.9–20%.

04Absent from the code

What the model leaves out

Each item below is absent from the code. A reviewer can ask about any of them.

Optics

No polarisation or phase encoding, no drift and no misalignment. Every channel error is a bit flip with probability p, independent of basis.

Detector effects

Missing are dark counts, afterpulsing, dead time and efficiency mismatch between detectors. A uniform detector efficiency is not missing: it multiplies the transmittance, so it is absorbed into η.

Source effects

The source is an ideal single-photon source. There are no multi-photon pulses, so no photon-number-splitting attack and no decoy states.

Finite-key effects

Key rate and threshold are asymptotic. The abort decision uses the point estimate Q̂ from a 2,000-bit sample. The Wilson interval is reported but does not enter the decision.

Other attacks

The only simulated attack is intercept-resend with a uniform random basis. There are no cloning attacks and no simulated collective or coherent attacks. Collective attacks appear only as an assumption of the rate formula. The classical channel is assumed authenticated and is not attacked.

Statistical edge cases

Every flip, loss and basis choice is independent and identically distributed per qubit. There are no burst errors, no time correlation and no basis-dependent error rates, so the Z and X QBERs are equal by construction.

05qiskit_demo.py

The Qiskit layer

qiskit_demo.build_circuit() runs the same protocol as quantum circuits on Qiskit Aer. Each BB84 round is one qubit. Dashed gates are applied only when the condition under them holds.

Aliceencode
|0⟩start Xif bit = 1 Hif basis X
Eveoptional
Hif basis X M→ eve register Hif basis X
Bobmeasure
Hif basis X M→ bob register

Eve's re-preparation happens through measurement collapse. After her measurement the qubit is in her measured state in the Z frame, and the second H, applied when her basis is X, rotates it back to her basis. Rounds are batched eight qubits per circuit and run with one shot each, using fixed simulator and transpiler seeds.

It is a cross-check only. No experiment depends on it. Circuit simulation cannot reach the sample sizes the statistics need, and for BB84's four states it adds nothing to correctness (section 1). It shows that the probability model and a circuit-level simulation give the same QBER.
